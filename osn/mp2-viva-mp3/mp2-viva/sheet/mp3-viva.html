Running a viva

  1. Run ./viva.py test beforehand (or x on the student) and read the Tests tab. Enter on a row shows its log.
  2. Check their push history and AI chat links (Commits and AI usage tabs), and the flags on the Overview tab.
  3. Try their programs yourself: c for the concurrency parts, v to boot their xv6.
  4. Ask for a high-level overview of the whole implementation: they should convince you they did it themselves. The Code tab shows what they changed, so you can pick what to quiz on.

Concurrency

Airport troubles

Marks 80

Run

Press x and read the Tests tab: C1 to C8 are worth 10 each, and Enter on a row shows the violating runs with input and output. Press c for a shell with their built program (it prints the path; ./airport below stands for it). ./viva.py airport in out checks any output.

printf '2\n0 Student 1\n0 Immigration officer 1\n' | ./airportBoth enter at 0 (Q23). She waits for the scan: approves at 3, leaves at 4
printf '1\n5 Immigration officer 1\n' | ./airportEmpty batch: enters at 5, approves at 6, leaves at 7
./airport < in > out; ./viva.py airport in outYour own input. Lists each constraint and what broke it

Code marks

  • C1: while she is in the hall nobody enters and no student leaves
  • C2: she waits for every student in the hall to scan, then approves them as one batch
  • C3: stamps only after approval; she leaves right after approving, even for an empty batch
  • C4: no leaving the hall before the stamp
  • C5: a bag is loaded only after its owner's stamp; the belt holds 2
  • C6: a student collects after leaving the hall, once their bag is on the belt
  • C7: every stamped student's bag is loaded and collected exactly once, and no other bag is loaded
  • C8: every action takes 1 tick, except scanning which takes 2. Also checks 3 scanners and 2 handlers
  • Every person is a thread. Each constraint is 10 marks

Look for

  • A row failing in 1 of 69 runs is a race, not a logic error: ask them to find it
  • airport extras has no marks attached: output format, same-tick order, not-sorted input, huge ids, clean exit
  • Flags on the Overview tab: usleep-style delays (may be penalised), pthread_barrier, atomics. Barriers and atomics are not allowed (Q11, Q17, Q22); semaphores are (Q10)
  • The doubts doc is linked on the assignment page: Q8, Q9, Q12 and Q23 settle the timing questions

Questions

  1. 1Walk me through one student thread from arrival to exit. Where can it block?Easy

    Entering (officer inside), waiting for a scanner, waiting for approval, leaving the hall (officer inside), and waiting for its bag. Use it to choose what to dig into.

  2. 2How does your logical clock work? How do you know nothing else can happen at this tick before moving on?

    Either tick by tick or jumping to the next event (Q6, Q14). The hard part is the same: every thread must have finished or blocked for the current tick before the clock moves, usually tracked with a count of runnable threads under a lock.

  3. 3How does the officer know everyone in the hall has scanned, including those waiting for a scanner?

    A count of students inside who have not finished scanning, updated under a lock. She waits on a condition variable until it reaches zero. Students who are queued for a scanner are in the count too.

  4. 4A student and the officer arrive on the same tick. What happens, and why?Medium

    Both can start entering (Q23). She only closes the hall once her enters has finished, one tick later, so that student is in the hall and she has to wait for their scan. The lines are printed in input order (Q12).

  5. 5Why while around pthread_cond_wait and not if?Easy

    Wakeups can be spurious, and another woken thread may have changed the state first. The condition has to be checked again after waking.

  6. 6When she leaves, several students are waiting to enter or leave. signal or broadcast?

    broadcast: all of them can go now. signal wakes one, and the rest would sleep until something else happens to wake them.

  7. 7How do you enforce 3 scanners and 2 belt slots? Why does a semaphore fit?

    A counting semaphore starts at the number of free units: sem_wait takes one and blocks at zero, sem_post returns one. A counter and a condition variable under a mutex also work. When is the unit released? When the action ends: the scanner at start + 2, the belt slot at collect + 1 (Q8).

  8. 8Can students, handlers and the belt deadlock?Hard

    No cycle exists. A bag on the belt belongs to a stamped student who can always collect it eventually. A student held in the hall by a visit is only waiting for her to leave, and her leaving depends on nothing on the belt side.

  9. 9How do your threads finish? How does a handler know there are no more bags?Medium

    They need an explicit end condition, such as a count of expected bags, and a wakeup for handlers still waiting when the last bag is done. Otherwise the program prints everything and then hangs.

  10. 10What is the difference between a mutex and a binary semaphore?Easy

    A mutex has an owner: the thread that locked it must unlock it. A semaphore is a counter that any thread may post, which is why it suits signalling between threads.

  11. 11Why is simulating time with usleep a poor idea here?Easy

    The times are symbolic. Sleeping hides ordering bugs behind timing instead of fixing them, and it makes the program slow. The assignment warns it may be penalised.

  12. 12The input is not sorted by time (Q7). What do you do, and how do you keep same-tick lines in order?

    A stable sort on time keeps input order among equal times, which is what Q12 needs.

xv6 symlinks

symlink and open

Marks 40

Run

symlinktest and usertests -q in the Tests tab cover both syscalls (20 + 20): the 40 marks need both to pass. They run with the template's copies, so editing those shows up as a flag. Press v to boot their xv6 and try things yourself.

symlinktesttest symlinks: ok, test O_CREATE on existing symlink: ok, test concurrent symlinks: ok
usertests -qALL TESTS PASSED: symlinks must not have broken anything else

Code marks

  • int symlink(char *target, char *path): returns 0, or -1 on failure. The target need not exist
  • open follows links, through chains, up to a depth of 10
  • O_NOFOLLOW opens the link itself

Look for

  • T_SYMLINK in kernel/stat.h; the target stored in the inode's data blocks
  • begin_op/end_op around symlink, and every error path in open releasing what it holds
  • If the kernel panics the note shows the panic line

Questions

  1. 1Where do you store the target path? How long can it be?Easy

    In the link's data blocks, with writei, and read back with readi. Its length is limited by MAXPATH, because argstr copies it into a buffer of that size.

  2. 2Walk me through open on a symlink.

    namei and ilock get the inode. While it is a T_SYMLINK and O_NOFOLLOW is not set: readi the target, iunlockput the link, namei the target, ilock it, and count the depth. Then carry on as for a normal file.

  3. 3Why release the link's lock before looking up its target?Hard

    namei takes inode locks along the path. In a cycle (a to b to a), still holding a's lock would mean sleeping forever on a lock you own. Holding one inode while locking another can also deadlock against another process.

  4. 4Why the depth limit? What happens when it is exceeded?Easy

    Links can form cycles, and a -> b -> a would loop forever. open gives up and returns -1.

  5. 5echo hi >> link opens with O_CREATE. What would the unmodified create() do with an existing symlink?Medium

    Return 0, so the open fails: it only accepts an existing inode when it is T_FILE or T_DEVICE. It has to let links through and open then follows them, and symlinktest checks the link stays a link.

  6. 6Why begin_op/end_op around symlink?

    It writes the new inode, a directory entry and a data block. They have to commit as one log transaction, so a crash cannot leave half a link.

  7. 7rm on a symlink: what gets removed?Easy

    The link. unlink works on the directory entry and never follows, because open is the only syscall that does.

  8. 8Hard link vs symlink: what happens to each after rm of the original?Easy

    A hard link is another directory entry for the same inode, so nlink stays above zero and the data remains. A symlink only holds a path, so it dangles.

  9. 9Why can a symlink point at nothing, when a hard link can't?Easy

    symlink only stores a string. link needs an existing inode to bump nlink on.

  10. 10A relative target: relative to what, in your code?Medium

    If they just call namei(target), the process's current directory. Linux resolves it against the directory holding the link. Worth seeing whether they know the difference.

xv6 symlinks

ln and readlink

Marks 20

Run

The Tests tab runs a short series of shell commands in a fresh boot: ln -s and readlink are worth 10 each, and Enter shows each command with the console. Press v to try it by hand.

echo hello > f1; ln -s f1 f2; cat f2hello through the link
echo world >> f2; cat f1hello, world: the write went through the link
readlink f2f1
readlink f1An error message: it is not a symlink
ln f1 f3; rm f1; cat f2; cat f3cat f2 fails (dangling); cat f3 still prints the file
readlink f2Still f1: a dangling link has a target

Code marks

  • ln -s target path makes a symlink; plain ln still makes a hard link. -s only ever comes as the second argument
  • readlink prints the path the link points to, one level only
  • On something that is not a symlink, readlink prints an error and exits non-zero

Look for

  • The non-zero exit cannot be seen from the xv6 shell, which ignores exit codes: read user/readlink.c
  • _readlink has to be in UPROGS. If not, the row shows exec readlink failed

Questions

  1. 1How does readlink get the path out?Easy

    open with O_RDONLY | O_NOFOLLOW, fstat to check it is T_SYMLINK, then a plain read. The link's data is the path.

  2. 2Why did readlink not need its own syscall?

    With O_NOFOLLOW, open returns the link's own inode, and reading it gives the target.

  3. 3readlink on a dangling link: what happens, and why?

    It prints the target. O_NOFOLLOW never looks at the target, so it does not matter that it is missing.

  4. 4How does ln tell which kind of link to make?Easy

    argc == 4 and argv[1] is -s call symlink(argv[2], argv[3]). Otherwise link(argv[1], argv[2]) as before.

Birthday boy

Bellman-Ford

Marks 10

Run

The Tests tab compares their output with BFS on 14 connected graphs, including a 2000-vertex path and more threads than vertices. Graphs with several threads run 3 times. Press c to try it by hand.

printf '6 7 0 4\n0 1\n0 2\n1 3\n2 3\n3 4\n1 2\n4 5\n' | ./bellman0 1 1 2 3 4

Code marks

  • V E N T then E lines of S D: zero-indexed, bidirectional, every edge counts 1
  • Prints V space-separated distances, newline-terminated (Q18)
  • Uses threads; only locks, condition variables and semaphores (Q22). A hand-written barrier is fine, pthread_barrier_t is not (Q17)
  • Only C (Q4)

Look for

  • The banned-API flags on the Overview tab. Several students used atomics or pthread_barrier_t

Questions

  1. 1What is shared between your threads, and how is it protected?Easy

    The distance array and whatever tracks whether a round changed anything. A lock per vertex, or one lock, around each read-compare-write.

  2. 2Two threads relax the same vertex at once. What goes wrong without a lock?Medium

    Check-then-set is not atomic, so a smaller distance can be overwritten by a larger one. It is also a data race, which is undefined behaviour in C.

  3. 3Why at most V-1 rounds? When can you stop early?Easy

    A shortest path has at most V-1 edges, and each round extends correct distances by at least one edge. Stop after a whole round with no change.

  4. 4Does it matter if a thread sees another thread's update within the same round?Hard

    No. Distances only ever decrease and are always the length of some real path, so seeing a fresher value can only reach the answer sooner.

  5. 5How do you synchronise the end of a round?

    A barrier built from a mutex and condition variable, or joining and respawning the threads each round. Whichever they did, the next round must not start until all threads have finished this one.

  6. 6The graph is unweighted. What is the better algorithm, and why?Easy

    BFS, O(V+E), against O(VE) for Bellman-Ford. It visits each edge a constant number of times instead of once per round.

Birthday boy

Performance report

Marks 20

Run

Marked by hand from the root README.md. Press o to see it rendered on Forgejo.

(read the README on Forgejo)Two plots and the observations

Code marks

  • Runtime against V at a fixed core count, averaged over several graphs with the same V and E
  • The expected complexity on the same plot (such as kVE) with the fitted k stated
  • Runtime against T with V and E fixed, averaged over different edge sets
  • Observations explaining the runtime-against-T plot
  • Every plot watermarked with their IIIT username (placement may vary, Q20)

Look for

  • AI may write the plotting scripts (Q3). The results and the explanation must be their own

Questions

  1. 1What does k in your fit represent? What are its units?

    Roughly the time for one edge relaxation in seconds, including locking overhead, if the curve is kVE.

  2. 2Why average over several edge sets?Easy

    The number of rounds depends on the graph's shape and the order of its edges, so a single graph can be far from typical.

  3. 3With early stopping, does your program really take time proportional to VE?Medium

    Usually not. Rounds track the graph's diameter, which is small for random graphs, so measurements often fall well below kVE. A long path comes close to it.

  4. 4Why does adding threads stop helping?

    The sequential part (Amdahl), contention on locks, cache lines bouncing between cores, and more threads than cores causing context switches.

  5. 5Could 2 threads be slower than 1? Why?Medium

    Yes. For a small graph the locking and coherence traffic can cost more than the parallel work saves.
